Error analysis, generalized

The Taxonomy Loop

How raw transcripts turn into a short, defensible list of named failure modes. Three phases: first discover and evidence modes by looping sample → open-code → axial-code; then run a deductive sweep for anything the open coding missed; then consolidate what survived and apply it to everything reviewed.

Sizing this in practice

  • Underlying corpus: at least ~10× your target reviewed-sample size. Cluster and stratified sampling need enough raw material to actually find structure in.
  • Total reviewed, across every batch: at least 100 distinct traces by the end.
  • Breadth batch size: 15–30 traces — big enough for a pattern to show itself, small enough to stay fast.
  • Narrow (hardening) batch size: 10–20 — smaller is fine, since the pool is already filtered to look similar.
  • Replacement: never. Track every trace_id you've reviewed, in either loop, and exclude it from every future batch.

Phase 1 of 3

Why this isn't an infinite loop

  • The inner loop never signals convergence on its own. It's built to search for instances of a mode you already have, so running out of a narrow batch only means that search succeeded or didn't — it says nothing about whether an undiscovered mode exists elsewhere.
  • A falling discovery rate only means something once sampling has actually been broad. A low rate from a narrow slice of the data just means you haven't looked elsewhere yet, not that nothing else is wrong.
  • A trace's second or third failure isn't lost — open-coding deliberately stops at the first one to stay fast. The rest surface later: either a deliberate re-read past that stopping point, or the exhaustive pass in Figure 3.

Three things this isn't

  • Not a prevalence estimate. The fractions in Figure 3 describe a review set you deliberately shaped with clustering and targeted search — not a random sample of the real population. Estimating true prevalence needs a separate, genuinely random sample of the whole corpus.
  • Not every undesirable behavior is automatically a "failure." Something can be bad for the user without yet being a defined rule. Decide explicitly whether to write it down as one before you count it — otherwise a judgment call quietly passes as an objective violation.
  • Not purely inductive. The deductive sweep starts from a hypothesis — a published category, or a requirement you haven't yet seen violated — and searches for confirming evidence, the reverse of Figure 1's data-first open coding. Reasoning in that direction invites confirmation bias; the unchanged evidentiary bar (≥3 positive, ≥3 close-negative, independently applicable) is what keeps it honest.
